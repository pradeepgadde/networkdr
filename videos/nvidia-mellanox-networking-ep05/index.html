0:52Melan Ox was founded in May 1999 in Yaknam, Israel by Isle Wald Man, a former executive at the Israeli chipmaker Galileo Technology together with a group of fellow engineers. That same year, the industry merged two competing proposals into a single new standard, Infiniband, overseen by the Infiniband Trade Association. It was designed as a high-speed, low latency fabric to connect servers and storage. Melon Ox bet the company on Infiniband silicon. It designed switch chips and host channel adapters, the network cards that connect servers to an infinib fabric. 1:48Melanox kept going, focusing on the one market where performance mattered above all, high performance computing. In February 2007, Melanox went public on NASDAQ, raising about $12 million. The key idea behind Infiniband is RDMA, remote direct memory access. In traditional networking, data passes through the operating systems network stack on both ends. Every message involves the CPU, the kernel, and several memory copies, adding latency and consuming processor cycles. With RDMA, the network adapter reads and writes application memory directly on both servers, bypassing the kernel and the CPU. Latency drops to around a microscond and the processors stay free for computation. For applications, sending data to another server starts to feel almost like reading local memory.

2:47That is why RDM became the foundation for parallel computing, distributed storage and later AI training. Infiniband also added lossless credit-based flow control and a central subnet manager. Later RDM came to Ethernet through Rocky RDM over converged Ethernet which became essential in cloud and AI data centers. Through the 2000s and 2010, Melanox became the standard interconnect for supercomputers. In 2011, Melanox bought Voltater, an Israeli maker of Infini band and Ethernet switches for about $218 million, becoming a complete system supplier rather than just a chip company. In 2013, it acquired Cotura and IPronics, adding silicon photonix and optical components. These became the link X line of cables and transceivers.

4:00By the early 2010, Infini Band connected a large share of the world's top 500 supercomputers, and Melon Ox had become the clear leader in higherformance interconnects. But supercomputing was a niche. The much bigger market was Ethernet in cloud data centers. Melanox's Connect X chips already supported Ethernet and its 25, 50, and 100 GB Ethernet adapters became popular with cloud providers, helped by Rocky. The bet was that the same low latency engineering that served supercomputers would matter in cloud data centers as storage moved to flash and applications became more distributed. In 2015, Melanox launched Spectrum, its own Ethernet switch chip and switch family, competing with Broadcombased switches. It promoted open Ethernet, letting customers run its own operating system or alternatives such as Cumulus Linux and Sonic.
