0:00Open up a switch from Arista, a white box running Sonic or many models from Cisco and Juniper, and there is a good chance you will find the same company's chip inside. That company is Broadcom. This is the story of how two very different companies, a UCL, a startup, and a spin-off of Huelet Packard's chip division, came together to become the most important supplier of networking silicon in the world and one of the most valuable companies on Earth. will follow six chapters, two origins, the merchant silicon revolution, Jericho and Deep Buffers, Avago's takeover and Hawkton's acquisition machine, VMware and Software, and the AI era of custom accelerators and 100 terab switches.

0:48The first origin is Broadcom Corporation. It was founded in August 1991 by Henry Samuelli, a UCL engineering professor and Henry Nicholas, one of his students, to build chips for broadband communication. Broadcom went public on NASDAQ in 1998 and grew into a leader in chips for cable modems, set top boxes, DSL, Ethernet, and later Wi-Fi and Bluetooth, which ended up in billions of smartphones. The second origin goes back much further to 1961 when Huelet Packard set up a semiconductor division called HP Associates. In 1999, it moved to the HP spin-off Agyant Technologies. In 2005, private equity firms KKR and Silverlake bought Agyant's chip business for $2.6 billion and named it Avago Technologies. In 2006, Hawkton became chief executive and in August 2009, Avago went public on NASDAQ.

1:55Broadcom Corporation entered the switch chip business in January 1999 when it bought Maverick Networks, a startup designing chips for multi-layer network switches for about $14 million. that became the Strata Switch and Strata XGS families, single chips that could do the switching work of an entire line card. Broadcom also sold Ethernet physical layer chips and network adapters, putting it inside a huge share of the world's Ethernet ports. In 2011, Broadcom bought Net Logic micro systemystems for $3.7 billion, adding knowledgebased processors used for fast route lookups and multi-core network processors. Broadcom's switch chips started in enterprise and lowcost data center switches. But a much bigger opportunity was about to open up.

2:49Traditionally, network vendors designed their own chips, wrote their own software, and built their own boxes, all tightly integrated. Broadcom offered another model, merchant silicon. One chip design sold to many vendors who could focus on software and system design instead of building custom chips for every product. Arista was built on this model from the start. Over time, Cisco, Juniper, HPE, Dell, and many others used Broadcom chips in some of their switches, and cloud providers bought white box switches from manufacturers in Taiwan and China running open software such as Sonic.

3:28Merchant silicon also changed the economics. A single Broadcom chip generation could ship in switches from dozens of brands, spreading the enormous cost of chip design across the whole industry. This disagregation transformed data center networking. Hardware became a fastmoving commodity and Broadcom became the common platform underneath much of the industry. Broadcom Strata XGS line split into families for different jobs. Trident chips targeted enterprise and data center switches with rich features.

4:04Trident Roman 2 in 2012 delivered 1.28 28 terabs per second and powered a generation of 10 and 40 Gbit data center switches. In 2014, Broadcom introduced Tomahawk built for the cloud with 3.2 tabs per second and support for 25, 50, and 100 GB Ethernet. Later, Trident generations added programmability so features could be updated in software. While Tomahawk focused on raw bandwidth and power efficiency for the largest cloud networks. From there, Tomahawk roughly doubled in capacity every 2 years or so. 6.4 tabs, then 12.8, 25.6, and 51.2 tabs with Tomahawk 5 in 2022.

4:53Each generation set the pace for the whole data center switching industry. Not every network wants the fastest possible chip. Routers and large modular switches need deep packet buffers and huge forwarding tables. In December 2009, Broadcom bought Dune Networks, an Israeli chip startup for $178 million. Its technology became the Strata DNx family with AAD and later Jericho launched in 2015. Jericho chips use external memory to hold gigabytes of packet buffers and millions of routes and they can be linked through fabric chips into large modular systems. They became the basis for many spine switches and routers built on merchant silicon including platforms from Arista and Cisco. Later generations added Jericho 3 AI for AI clusters and in August 2025 Jericho 4 which uses high bandwidth memory for buffering and can connect AI data centers more than 100 km apart.

6:01Meanwhile, Hawkton was turning Avago into an acquisition machine. His strategy was to buy companies with leading technology franchises, cut costs sharply, sell or close businesses that were not market leaders, and invest heavily in the ones that were. On May 28, 2015, Avago announced its biggest deal yet, the acquisition of Broadcom Corporation for $ 37 billion in cash and stock. The deal closed on February 1, 2016. The combined company took the Broadcom name and kept the AVgo ticker.

6:38Broadcom's founders Samu Elli and Nicholas had built one of the great communications chip companies and Samu Ellie joined the new board. True to form, Broadcom sold noncore units such as its internet of things business, sold to Cyprus for $550 million, and doubled down on networking, broadband, and wireless. The acquisitions kept coming. In 2014, Avago had bought LSI, a storage chipmaker, for 6.6 billion and PLX technology, whose PCI Express switches connect processors inside servers. In 2015, it bought Emullex and in 2017, Briade, the leader in fiber channel storage networking. Each deal followed a similar playbook. focus on the largest customers, invest in the most critical products, and cut costs everywhere else.

7:34In November 2017, Broadcom moved its legal home from Singapore to the United States. That same month, it launched an unsolicited bid for Qualcomm valued at more than $100 billion. In March 2018, the US government blocked the deal on national security grounds. Broadcom then turned to software. It bought CA Technologies for $18.9 billion in 2018 and Semantex Enterprise Security Business for 10 $7 billion in 2019. In May 2022, Broadcom announced its largest acquisition ever, VMware, the leader in server virtualization.

8:19The deal closed on November 22, 2023, valued at about $69 billion. Broadcom quickly reorganized VMware around VMware Cloud Foundation, moved customers from perpetual licenses to subscriptions, and cut its product line, a shift that angered many customers, but sharply increased profitability. It also sold parts of VMware. The end-user computing business went to KKR for $4 billion in 2024 and the VOC cloud SDWAN business went to Arista in 2025.

8:57Infrastructure software is now a major part of Broadcom. In the third quarter of fiscal 2026, it brought in $ 8.75 billion, about 30% of total revenue. Then came AI and Broadcom's networking business found itself at the center of the largest buildout in data center history. In June 2025, Broadcom began shipping Tomahawk 6, the first switch chip with 102.4 terabts per second built for clusters of 100,000 to a million accelerators with a co-acked optics version. In July 2025, Tomahawk Ultra targeted scaleup networks inside AI racks with very low latency and Broadcom proposed its scaleup Ethernet framework as an open alternative to proprietary interconnects like NVLink. Broadcom was also a founding member of the ultraethernet consortium and argues that open standard Ethernet should connect every part of an AI cluster. In October 2025, it introduced Thor Ultra, an 800 GB AI network card compliant with the Ultra Ethernet specification.

10:13Broadcom now offers chips for every layer of the AI network, scale up, scale out, scale across, and the network card. Broadcom's other AI business is custom accelerators, which it calls XPS. For about a decade, Broadcom has worked with Google on its tensor processing units, and it designs custom AI chips for several other large customers, combining their architectures with Broadcom's chip design, packaging, and high-speed connectivity. Each program is a deep multi-year partnership. The customer defines the architecture and Broadcom brings the process technology, advanced packaging, memory interfaces, and the high-speed SDS that move data on and off the chip. In October 2025, Open AI and Broadcom announced a partnership to develop 10 gawatts of custom AI accelerators and rack systems with deployment starting in the second half of 2026 and continuing through 2029 connected with Ethernet. Custom accelerators and networking together make Broadcom the main alternative to Nvidia for companies that want to build their own AI infrastructure.

11:29The results have been extraordinary. In fiscal 2025, Broadcom's revenue reached about $63.9 billion. In the third quarter of fiscal 2026, revenue was a record $29.6 billion, up 86% and AI semiconductor revenue was $16.7 billion, up 221%. For the fourth quarter, Broadcom guided to about $34.8 $8 billion in revenue, including 217 billion from AI chips. Networking remains a large part of that AI business because every new cluster of custom accelerators or GPS needs switches, network cards, and optics to connect it. Its market value passed $1 trillion in December 2024 and $2 trillion in April 2026, making Broadcom one of the most valuable companies in the world.

12:30Broadcom is above all a company built by acquisitions. Before 2016, Broadcom Corporation bought Maverick Networks, Dune Networks, and Net Logic, the foundations of its networking chips. Avago assembled a semiconductor powerhouse with SCI optics, LSI, PLX, MUX, Broadcom itself, and Briade. Then came the software era with CA, Semantex, Enterprise Business, and VMware along with sales of businesses it did not want to keep.

13:03Broadcom's switch chips tell their own story from 1.28 terabs per second with Trident Roman 2 in 2012 to 102.4 4 terabs with Tomahawk 6 in 2025, an 80fold increase in 13 years. Its key networking lines include the Trident and Tomahawk switch chips, the Jericho and Kumron deep buffer chips, Thor network cards, PCI Express switches, and custom AI accelerators. Broadcom competes with Nvidia in AI networking and accelerators with Marvel in custom silicon and networking chips and with Cisco's silicon 1 in switch silicon. From a UCLA startup and an HP chip division to the silicon underneath the AI era, Broadcom is the company that made merchant networking possible.
