0:50Aruba Networks was founded in February 2002 in Sunavail by Kir Melcote and Pun Kaj Mlick. Both were experienced networking engineers and they had seen how slowly enterprise networks were adapting to laptops and mobile users. At the time, Wi-Fi in businesses was mostly an afterthought. Access points were installed one by one, each configured on its own, and IT departments worried that wireless was insecure and impossible to manage at scale. Aruba's founders believed that mobility would change that. Employees would expect to work from anywhere in a building and eventually from anywhere at all, and the network would need to follow them securely. The company raised venture funding and set out to build a wireless system designed for large enterprises rather than for homes and small offices.

1:45Aruba's answer was the controller-based architecture. In the early model, every access point was autonomous. Each one ran its own software, stored its own configuration, and had to be updated separately. So, a large campus could mean thousands of devices to manage by hand. Aruba used thin access points connected through tunnels to a central mobility controller. The controller handled configuration security policy, roaming, and radio management for the whole network. Aruba also built a stateful firewall into the controller, applying policy to each user based on identity and role rather than just to ports and addresses. It called this identity based networking because the controller saw every user and every radio. It could also tune channels and power automatically and steer devices to less crowded access points which mattered more and more as networks grew denser. Cisco which had bought airspace in 2005 adopted a similar controller model and the two companies became the main rivals in enterprise Wi-Fi for the next decade.
